PIT ENGINE / 03

Information timing comes before factor design

Point-in-time handling is more than a shift(1). It tracks when events take effect, when every dependency is known, and how those observations map to trading dates.

01

Preserve first releases

For each stock and reporting period, retain the first available release. Later revisions must not overwrite the historical record.

02

Calculate by reporting period

Calculate year-on-year changes, differences and trends on the reporting-period sequence before mapping them to daily observations.

03

Backward as-of

A signal may use only observations effective on or before its date. Future releases cannot be backfilled.

04

Defer after-close releases

Information released after the close becomes eligible on the next trading day, not in the same day’s closing signal.

05

Track dependency timing

A composite measure becomes available only when its last required input is known.

06

Match forecast periods

Expectation gaps and three-month revisions compare the same fiscal forecast period using consistent definitions.

VALIDATION / 05

Missing data does not lower the validation standard

Newly released information must not alter past results. The API rejects records with incomplete provenance, invalid timestamps or synthetic-data flags.

    DATA BOUNDARIES / 06

    What QMT can—and cannot—supply

    • Available QMT market prices, turnover, holdings and downloaded financial fields may enter the data pipeline.
    • Onshore and offshore consensus forecasts, report titles, rating upgrades and shared-analyst links require separate point-in-time datasets. They remain unavailable when QMT does not supply them.
    • The site does not fill these gaps with a fixed sample, random values or substitute public-market data.
    • The site is read-only. It neither reads a trading account nor submits orders.
